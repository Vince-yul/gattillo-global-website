# Call Sheet — concept rationale

## Concept
A trade paper, not a stage. Where Premiere Red is a dark theatre with a marquee, Call Sheet is a warm paper masthead: ivory ground, near-black ink, one brass accent. It reads like the front page of the trade press this audience already trusts — Baskerville-scale headlines, hairline rules, roman-numeral ledgers instead of app-style cards.

## Job to be done
Same brief as Premiere Red: convince a rights holder, operator, manufacturer, or capital partner that Gattillo is a serious counterparty. Call Sheet leans on institutional trust cues (paper, serif, restraint) rather than theatrical ones (dark stage, marquee glow). Built as a genuine second direction to compare side by side, not a reskin.

## What's new versus Premiere Red
All existing copy is preserved verbatim. Two new sections were added because a second pass is a chance to say more, not just look different:
- **Focus Areas** — six concrete verticals (theme parks, location-based entertainment, licensed products, pro sports/talent, manufacturing & distribution, international expansion) so a visitor can place themselves faster than the abstract capability list alone allows.
- **How We Work** — a short FAQ answering the questions a serious prospect asks before the first email: what Gattillo charges for, what stage to reach out at, territories, and what's needed to start. Deliberately generic on economics (no invented figures or clients) — it describes the engagement model, not fabricated specifics.

## Typography
Libre Baskerville (regular only) for every headline, run large with no faux-bold — the scale carries the weight instead of a heavy cut. General Sans for body/UI, reused from Premiere Red's font set for shared brand DNA across concepts.

## Numbering
Roman numerals (I–VI) throughout the ledger-style lists — the concept's signature, distinct from Premiere Red's plain 01–06.

## Photography
The same hero floor photo is reused once, in the "Gattillo Difference" section, duotone-treated (grayscale + sepia + luminosity blend) into the brass/paper palette rather than shown as a raw color hero — keeps the page feeling like paper even where a photo appears.
